## Why Dig to Escape Classes Matter More Than You Think

Dig to Escape classes are the biggest permanent power spike in the game, and buying the wrong one can quietly cost you hundreds of Diamonds. Every run comes down to the same three bottlenecks: how fast you dig, how long your energy lasts, and how much the shop drains from your wallet. A good class deletes one of those bottlenecks for good. A bad one adds a bonus you will never actually notice.

That is why a Dig to Escape classes tier list is worth reading before you spend a single Diamond. Unlike tools or consumables, classes are not one-run purchases — they stick to your account forever, which means every Diamond you put into a weak class is a Diamond you cannot put into a strong one. Since Diamonds only trickle in from completed and failed runs, the order you buy in matters as much as what you buy.

Here is the short version of what each class actually fixes:

| What keeps slowing you down | Class that fixes it | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Getting around the map takes forever | Speedster | 100 Diamonds |
| Energy bar empties mid-dig | Energizer | 250 Diamonds |
| Inventory fills up too fast | Heavy Lifter | 400 Diamonds |

| Shop prices eat your savings | Bargainer | 900 Diamonds |

## How to Unlock Every Class in Dig to Escape

Classes are not something you find underground — they are bought in the main lobby before a run starts. Once you own a class, it stays unlocked permanently, and you can equip it before any future escape. The catch is that you can only wear one at a time, so unlocking all five is about flexibility, not stacking bonuses.

| Step | What to do | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Earn Diamonds by finishing runs | Payout scales with how far you got, so failed attempts still pay |
| 2 | Find the Classes NPC in the lobby | This is the only place to browse costs and effects |
| 3 | Buy the class you want | Stronger classes cost more Diamonds |
| 4 | Equip it before starting a run | Only one class can be active at a time |

A quick note on earning: both successful escapes and failed attempts reward Diamonds based on progress, so a run that ends at the bottom of the map still moves you closer to your next unlock. If you are grinding toward an expensive class, push depth over a risky escape attempt — the payout is tied to how much you accomplished, not just whether you made it out.

If you are new to the platform itself, make sure your client is current through the [official Roblox website](https://www.roblox.com/) before troubleshooting any in-game purchase issues, since lobby NPCs occasionally fail to load on outdated versions.

## Dig to Escape Classes Tier List

This ranking comes from community reports and player experience across different escape routes. Your personal best class will depend on the ending you are chasing, whether you play solo or with friends, and how much you value speed versus safety.

| Tier | Classes |
| --- | --- |
| S Tier | Bargainer |

### S Tier: Bargainer

Bargainer takes 20% off every item in the shop, and because you buy food, tools, and consumables constantly during a run, that discount stacks up across dozens of purchases. It is the only class that improves your economy rather than your movement or digging, which makes it the strongest long-term pick in the game — and the most expensive at 900 Diamonds.

The downside is timing. Buying Bargainer early means delaying Energizer or Hyperactive, and those classes make the actual digging noticeably smoother. Treat Bargainer as the class you buy once you are already comfortable escaping, not the one you buy to learn with.

### A Tier: Energizer and Hyperactive

Energizer cuts energy consumption while digging by 20%. In practice that means more blocks cleared per energy bar, fewer food purchases, and fewer trips back toward the surface to recover. It shines on layer-heavy routes where you are chewing through terrain for a long stretch without a break.

Hyperactive reduces dig cooldown by 25%, which is the most direct speed increase available — every single dig animation resolves faster, so progress compounds over hundreds of blocks. It is expensive at 750 Diamonds, but the improvement is immediately obvious the moment you equip it.

### B Tier: Speedster

Speedster gives a flat 25% movement speed boost for only 100 Diamonds, which makes it the best value purchase in the game if you are just starting out. It will not help you dig faster, and it will not save you money, but it cuts down the dead time spent walking between the surface, the shop, and your tunnel.

For speedrunners and players learning the map layout, it is genuinely great. For anyone deep into a grinding routine, the bonus fades into the background once you own a digging-focused class.

### C Tier: Heavy Lifter

Heavy Lifter adds 20% carry capacity for 400 Diamonds, and that is where the value stops. Inventory space is rarely the real limit in a run because you can dig straight down and return to the surface whenever you want, and the extra slots only matter if you are deliberately minimizing trips.

Energizer solves the same "I keep having to go back up" problem more efficiently by reducing how often you need to resupply in the first place. In multiplayer, Heavy Lifter looks even weaker, since teammates split the loot and no single player's inventory fills quickly.

## Which Class Should You Buy First?

Buying order matters more than most players expect, because Diamonds come in slowly and the gap between a 100-Diamond class and a 900-Diamond class is enormous. Here is a practical progression path based on community experience.

| Stage | Diamonds available | Recommended purchase | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early | 0–250 | Speedster | Cheap, makes learning the map much less tedious |
| Early-mid | 250–500 | Energizer | Fewer resupply trips, better deep-run consistency |
| Mid | 500–800 | Hyperactive | Biggest raw digging speed increase |
| Late | 800+ | Bargainer | Discount pays for itself across many future runs |
| Optional | Any | Heavy Lifter | Buy last, or skip entirely |

A few practical tips that make the order work better:

- **Do not rush the expensive classes.** A 900-Diamond purchase made too early means dozens of runs with no class equipped at all.
- **Match the class to the ending.** Layer-heavy routes reward Energizer; short, sprint-style escapes reward Speedster.
- **Re-equip before every run.** It is easy to forget after switching classes while experimenting.
- **Play with friends when testing.** A class that feels weak solo can be more or less useful in a group, especially Heavy Lifter.

If you want a single recommendation: grab Speedster first, then Energizer, then Hyperactive, and finish with Bargainer once your Diamond income is steady.

## Dig to Escape Classes FAQ

**How many classes are in Dig to Escape?**
There are five: Speedster, Energizer, Heavy Lifter, Hyperactive, and Bargainer. Each one costs a different amount of Diamonds and provides a single distinct bonus.

**Can you equip more than one class at a time?**
No. You can unlock all five permanently, but only one class can be active during a run, so choose based on the escape route you are attempting.

**What is the best class for beginners?**
Speedster. At 100 Diamonds it is the cheapest unlock in the game, and the 25% movement boost makes learning the map far less frustrating. Upgrade to Energizer or Hyperactive once you have more Diamonds banked.

**Is this Dig to Escape classes tier list official?**
No. It is based on player experience and community reports rather than developer statements, and rankings can shift after balance updates. Treat it as a starting point and adjust based on which escape routes you actually enjoy running.
