## Dig to Escape Controls at a Glance

Almost every escape comes down to six inputs. Learn them once and you will stop looking down at your keyboard.

| Action | Default Input | What It Does |
|---|---|---|
| Move | W, A, S, D | Walks your character in any direction; also the input that drives digging |
| Interact / pick up | E | Opens doors, grabs tools, scans keycards, picks up items |
| Sprint | Hold Shift | Runs while held — essential for long corridors and chase sequences |
| Jump | Spacebar | Clears low obstacles, gaps, and planks |
| Camera / look | Mouse movement | Rotates your view independently of your movement direction |
| Dig | Push into soft terrain | Removes the block ahead of your character's center point |

The one input people overlook is the camera. Your mouse does not move your character — it moves your eyes. In tight tunnels that distinction matters, because you can keep sprinting forward while turning the camera to check for side passages or patrolling guards.

## How Digging Actually Works

Digging in this game is not a separate button. Community reports on the Roblox developer forum describe the system as a center-point model: the exact spot your character occupies removes the tile directly in front of it, while the surrounding tiles break away in a wider pattern around that center. In practice, that means your body position is your digging tool.

A few consequences follow from that design:

- **Face the wall you want gone.** Walking into a corner digs the corner, not the wall beside it.
- **Small adjustments beat big swings.** Nudging your character sideways clears a wider tunnel than running in a straight line.
- **Stop moving and you stop digging.** Releasing the movement keys halts progress instantly, which is useful when you need to listen for guards.

According to [IGN's Dig to Escape endings guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/dig-to-escape/Endings_Guide_-_How_to_Unlock_All_Endings), a progress bar appears on the right side of your screen once you begin digging beneath your cell. That bar is your best feedback tool: if it is not filling, you are not actually in contact with diggable terrain.

| Digging Signal | What It Means | What To Do |
|---|---|---|
| Progress bar filling | You are actively removing terrain | Keep holding the movement key |
| Progress bar stalled | You hit a hard block or an object | Adjust your angle or find another route |
| Tiles crumbling around you | Center-point spread is working | Continue forward, do not back up |
| Bar disappears | You left the dig zone | Return to the soft terrain |

## Dig to Escape Controls on PC, Mobile, and Console

The game runs on Roblox, so your input method changes more than the underlying rules do. If you are switching between devices, expect a short adjustment period — especially for camera control.

| Platform | Movement | Interact | Sprint | Camera |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PC (keyboard + mouse) | W, A, S, D | E | Hold Shift | Mouse |
| Mobile (touch) | On-screen joystick | Tap button | Hold sprint button | Drag screen |
| Console (gamepad) | Left stick | Face button prompt | Click left stick or hold | Right stick |

On mobile, the single biggest upgrade you can make is rearranging your touch layout. Roblox lets you reposition and resize the on-screen buttons, and moving the sprint and interact buttons closer to your thumbs cuts reaction time noticeably. On console, camera speed is the usual culprit when players feel sluggish — bump the sensitivity up before blaming the game.

One more platform note: if you play on PC with a controller plugged in, Roblox sometimes registers both input devices at once and your character stutters. Unplug the controller or disable it in settings to fix that.

## Movement Techniques That Shave Seconds Off Your Run

Raw keybinds only get you so far. These habits are what separate a first clear from a fast one.

| Technique | How To Do It | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| Sprint jump | Jump while holding Shift | Covers gaps without losing forward momentum |
| Camera pre-turn | Turn the camera before you reach a corner | You commit to the correct direction instantly |
| Wall hug | Move along a wall while digging | Keeps your center point on the shortest path |
| Stop-and-listen | Release movement keys in new areas | Digging noise can mask approaching threats |
| Deliberate jumping | Jump only at obstacles, not constantly | Constant jumping is slower than a clean sprint |

Insider Gaming's Kitchen ending guide makes the same point about obstacle sections — keep running forward and clear gaps, planks, and slow-down zones, but jump deliberately rather than spamming the key. Every jump costs you a fraction of forward speed, and those fractions add up across a long escape route.

Practice the sprint jump in an empty area before you rely on it. The timing window is generous, but it is not the same as jumping from a standstill, and mistiming it over a gap usually ends the run.

## Troubleshooting Dig to Escape Controls

Most "broken controls" reports turn out to be one of five fixable issues. Work through this table before assuming the game is bugged.

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Character will not move | Chat box or menu has focus | Click the game window or press Escape |
| Shift does nothing | Shift Lock conflict or stuck key | Check Roblox settings, toggle Shift Lock off |
| E does nothing | Too far from the object, or wrong item equipped | Step closer; equip the required tool first |
| Camera spins wildly | Mouse sensitivity too high | Lower sensitivity in Roblox settings |
| Buttons overlap on mobile | Default touch layout | Reposition buttons in the Roblox mobile menu |

A quick note on Shift Lock: it is a Roblox-level camera setting, not a game mechanic, and some players report that leaving it on makes sprinting feel inconsistent. If your sprint keeps dropping out, toggle it and test again.

If none of those help, leave the server and rejoin. Input desync is rare, but it happens, and a fresh session clears it faster than troubleshooting ever will.

## A Five-Minute Warm-Up Routine

Before you attempt a serious run, spend five minutes drilling the basics:

1. Sprint in a straight line for ten seconds, then stop on command.
2. Dig a short tunnel, then reverse direction without turning the camera.
3. Practice three sprint jumps over a gap.
4. Interact with two different objects back to back.

Players who warm up report far fewer panicked misclicks in the first minute of a run, which is exactly when most escapes fall apart.

## FAQ About Dig to Escape Controls

**What are the default dig to escape controls on PC?**
W, A, S, and D move your character, E interacts and picks up objects, holding Shift sprints, Spacebar jumps, and the mouse controls your camera. Digging itself is triggered by pushing your character into soft terrain rather than pressing a dedicated key.

**How do I sprint in Dig to Escape?**
Hold Shift while moving. It is a hold, not a toggle, so releasing the key drops you back to walking speed. On mobile, hold the on-screen sprint button; on console, click or hold the left stick depending on your layout.

**Why is my interact key not working?**
The two most common reasons are distance and equipment. Step closer to the door, terminal, or item, and make sure you are holding the correct tool — a keycard scan will not trigger if the keycard is not equipped.

**Can I change the controls?**
Roblox does not offer full key rebinding for most experiences, but you can adjust mouse sensitivity, camera mode, and Shift Lock in the platform settings. Mobile players get the most flexibility, since the touch layout is fully customizable.

Get the basics into muscle memory and the rest of the game opens up. The controls are simple on purpose — the challenge is meant to come from routing, timing, and nerve. Once your hands stop thinking about which key to press, you can finally start thinking about where to dig next.
