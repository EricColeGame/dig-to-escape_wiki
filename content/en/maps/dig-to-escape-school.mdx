## The Core Escape Loop: Dig, Carry, Upgrade, Repeat

Every run follows the same four-beat rhythm. You dig blocks out of the school's foundation, carry them away, spend the rewards on upgrades, and then dig faster than you did before. The game's own description names three upgrade tracks — block-carrying power, shovel quality, and movement speed — plus teamwork as the multiplier on top of all three.

The trick is that these four things are not equally valuable at every moment. Early on, your bottleneck is how slowly blocks break. Mid-run, your bottleneck becomes the walk between your tunnel and your drop-off point. Late in a run, the bottleneck is usually coordination, because several players carving in different directions waste effort on tunnels nobody finishes.

| Upgrade track | What it changes | Problem it solves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Block-carrying power | How many blocks you can haul per trip | Constant backtracking to unload |
| Shovel upgrades | How quickly blocks break | Slow, unsatisfying digging |
| Speed | How fast you travel between tunnel and drop-off | Long, boring walks |
| Teamwork | How fast a shared hole gets cleared | Solo grinding in a crowded server |

A repeatable breakout routine looks like this:

| Step | Action | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Join the group and like the game | Free bonus before you start |
| 2 | Pick one lane and commit to it | Prevents wasted, half-finished tunnels |
| 3 | Dig until your carry limit is full | Keeps a steady reward flow |
| 4 | Deposit and immediately buy shovel upgrades | Fixes your earliest bottleneck |
| 5 | Extend the same lane deeper | Compounds progress instead of restarting |
| 6 | Bank toward carry capacity and speed | Cuts dead travel time out of the loop |

Following that order in a fresh server is the single fastest way to dig to escape school without wasting early currency on upgrades that don't pay off yet.

## Upgrade Priorities: What to Buy First

Most players who stall out underground have the same problem: they spread currency across every upgrade track at once. That feels balanced, but it isn't efficient. Incremental simulators reward you for fixing your current bottleneck completely before moving to the next one.

| Priority | Upgrade | Buy it when | Payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Shovel quality | Immediately, from your first payout | Every future block breaks faster |
| 2 | Block-carrying power | When trips to deposit feel frequent | Fewer interruptions per block |
| 3 | Speed | Once carrying and digging feel smooth | Shorter travel on every trip |
| 4 | Repeat in that order | Each time a track plateaus | Keeps progress compounding |

A simple rule of thumb: if you spend more time walking than digging, buy speed or carry capacity. If you spend more time staring at an unbroken block than moving, buy shovel upgrades. If you can't tell which is worse, buy shovel — it's the upgrade that multiplies everything else, because faster digging means faster income for every other purchase.

Also remember that upgrades persist in usefulness only while you keep digging. Sitting in the spawn area to admire your new shovel is the most common way players lose minutes per session without noticing.

## Team Play on a 30-Player Server

The game's description explicitly invites you to work together as a team, and on a full server that advice is worth real time. Voice chat makes coordination practical, but even without a mic you can contribute by picking a role and sticking to it.

| Role | What you do | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digger | Breaks blocks at the tunnel face | Players with shovel upgrades |
| Hauler | Carries blocks out and deposits them | Players with high carry capacity |
| Sprinter | Handles long-distance runs and scouting | Players with speed upgrades |
| Coordinator | Calls direction and keeps everyone in one lane | Voice chat users |
| Booster | Joins the group, likes the game, keeps morale up | New players |

Player experience suggests that the biggest team gains come from agreeing on a single direction rather than each person digging their own shaft. Two players in one tunnel clear it twice as fast and both get to the exit sooner; two players in separate tunnels just create two slower holes. If your server is chaotic, type a short direction call in chat or use voice chat to rally people — it costs five seconds and can save the whole lobby several minutes.

## Reading the Map: What to Watch For

Map knowledge in this style of game is less about memorizing a layout and more about reading your surroundings. Community reports consistently point to a few habits that separate fast escapers from slow ones.

| What to look for | Why it helps |
| --- | --- |
| Where other players are digging | Joining an active tunnel beats starting a fresh one |
| Dead-end pockets | Recognize them early and back out instead of clearing them |
| Shared drop-off routes | Deposit points near the group keep everyone moving |
| Open space above your tunnel | Confirms you're making real upward or outward progress |
| New players near spawn | Point them toward the group lane so their effort counts |

Because the school's structure is a shared space, your map sense is really a sense of where collective effort is concentrated. Follow the crowd's direction unless you have a specific reason not to, and avoid tunneling into a corner where your blocks help nobody escape.

## Mistakes That Keep Players Underground

Even players who understand the loop make the same handful of errors. Here's how to spot and fix them.

| Mistake | What it costs you | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spreading upgrades evenly | Slow progress on every track | Fix one bottleneck at a time |
| Digging alone in a full server | Wasted effort on duplicate tunnels | Join the group's lane |
| Ignoring the free group bonus | Missing permanent early value | Join the group and like the game |
| Never depositing | No income, no upgrades | Deposit whenever you're full |
| Quitting a run early | Lost compounding progress | Push one more upgrade tier |

The theme behind all five is the same: dig to escape school rewards consistency more than intensity. A player who digs steadily for ten minutes with a clear upgrade order will beat a player who digs frantically for ten minutes with no plan every single time.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to dig to escape school?**
It depends on server size, upgrade choices, and how well your team coordinates. Sessions with several players sharing one tunnel progress noticeably faster than solo attempts, and returning players who already know their upgrade order escape quicker than first-timers.

**Do I need voice chat to play?**
No. Voice chat helps with coordination, but chat messages and simply following the crowd's direction work fine. Pick a role, stick to one lane, and you'll contribute either way.

**What should I upgrade first?**
Shovel quality. Faster digging generates income faster, which funds carry capacity and speed sooner. Once walking becomes your main time sink, switch to carry power and speed.

**Is the group bonus worth it?**
Yes. The game page states that joining the official group and liking the experience both grant in-game bonuses. It takes seconds and applies for the rest of your session, so do it before you start digging.

Get the upgrade order right, dig in a shared lane, and deposit on every trip — that's the whole formula. Do it consistently and you'll spend far less time underground wondering why everyone else already escaped.
