## How Endings Work in Dig to Escape

Endings in Dig to Escape are not random. Each one is tied to a specific exit point that only becomes available once you satisfy a condition — either reaching a depth milestone, or holding a particular item when you arrive. The game remembers which ending you triggered, which is why so many players replay the map just to fill in the gaps on their ending list.

Two routes are consistently documented across community wikis and guides: the **Mineshaft** ending and the **Rooftops** ending. Other endings get mentioned in comment sections and Discord threads, but those two are the ones with repeatable, verifiable steps. Start there before you chase rumors.

| Ending | Exit Point | Core Requirement | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mineshaft | Bottom of the prison dig site | Reach the lowest depth and survive the final run | Low — no purchases needed |
| Rooftops | Prison roof | Save 300 Toilet Paper, buy the Illegal Keycard (200 Toilet Paper) | Medium — currency grind plus traversal |

The key difference is economy. Mineshaft is a pure skill-and-persistence route. Rooftops is a shopping list. If you are brand new, do Mineshaft first — it teaches you the digging loop that funds everything else.

## The Mineshaft Ending: Your First Attempt

The Mineshaft is the default ending, which is why most players get it by accident. You dig down through the prison floor layers, hit the bottom of the map, and trigger a scripted run event that ends with your escape. Simple on paper, easy to fumble in practice.

The mistake almost everyone makes is digging sideways. A wide, wandering tunnel wastes time, splits your attention, and leaves you far from the vertical shaft you need to get back up. Dig straight down, keep your inventory clear by selling what you pull up, and you will hit the trigger depth much faster.

| Step | What To Do | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Grab a digging tool at spawn | You cannot break floor layers without one |
| 2 | Dig a straight vertical shaft downward | Keeps your route short and easy to retrace |
| 3 | Sell dug-up items as your inventory fills | Prevents weight and slot problems mid-run |
| 4 | Watch for the depth trigger | The run event will not fire early |
| 5 | Sprint the moment the run starts | The event is timed; stopping for loot costs you |
| 6 | Cross the finish line | This locks in the Mineshaft ending |

### Tips for a Clean Mineshaft Run

- Do not fight other players on the way down. PvP delays you far more than it gains you.
- Drop heavy, low-value items before the run event so you are not slowed down.
- Learn the run route once at low stakes — the path is the same every time.
- If you fail the run, you keep your progress. Just dig back to the trigger and try again.

## The Rooftops Ending: Toilet Paper, the Illegal Keycard, and the Roof

Rooftops is where most players get stuck, and it is almost always a currency problem rather than a skill problem. According to the [IGN Dig to Escape endings guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/dig-to-escape/Endings_Guide_-_How_to_Unlock_All_Endings), the route works like this: dig up items, sell them, bank 300 Toilet Paper, purchase the Illegal Keycard for 200 Toilet Paper, then use that keycard to reach the rooftop and finish the escape.

Toilet Paper is the game's currency, and it drops from the items you excavate. That means the Rooftops ending is really a farming challenge wrapped in a parkour challenge.

| Requirement | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Toilet Paper on hand | 300 | You need the full amount saved before shopping |
| Illegal Keycard | 200 Toilet Paper | The mandatory purchase for this route |
| Leftover buffer | 100 Toilet Paper | Recommended for tools or upgrades after buying |

### Where To Spend Toilet Paper First

If you are still short on funds, the order you spend matters more than how fast you farm.

| Priority | Purchase | Cost | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Illegal Keycard | 200 Toilet Paper | Hard requirement for the Rooftops ending |
| 2 | Digging upgrades | Varies | Faster breaks mean faster farming if you are short |
| 3 | Mobility or stamina gear | Varies | Helps with the rooftop section of the route |

Community reports suggest that players who buy cosmetics first tend to stall for a long time on this ending. Resist the urge. The keycard is the gate, and everything else is a detour.

Once you have the keycard, head to the locked access point, clear the rooftop traversal, and reach the extraction zone. If you die on the roof, you keep the keycard — you just have to climb again.

## Fastest Order To Unlock Dig to Escape All Endings

Order matters more than most players realize. Doing Rooftops first means farming currency with no map knowledge and no upgraded tools — the slowest possible start. Here is the order that wastes the least time.

| Order | Ending | Prep Needed | Why This Order Works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mineshaft | A basic digging tool | Teaches the dig loop and run mechanic at zero cost |
| 2 | Rooftops | 300 Toilet Paper, Illegal Keycard | Easier once you know the map and have spare tools |

Between runs, sell everything you dig up. Even on a Mineshaft attempt, the items you pull out of the floor are funding your next Rooftops attempt. Treat every dig as a farming session with a bonus ending at the end.

## Common Mistakes That Slow Down Dig to Escape All Endings

After reading through community threads and guide comments, the same problems come up again and again:

- **Digging sideways instead of down.** It feels productive, but it stretches your route and slows the depth trigger.
- **Spending Toilet Paper on cosmetics early.** The keycard comes first, always.
- **Buying the keycard before saving the full amount.** Keep the full 300 banked so you are not caught short.
- **Panicking during the run event.** It is scripted and the path does not change. Learn it once.
- **Assuming the roof is inaccessible.** Players frequently walk past the access point because it looks blocked.
- **Ignoring updates.** A patch can change prices or add routes, so check the shop and patch notes after any update.

The single biggest time-saver is patience with your own learning curve. Two or three deliberate Mineshaft runs will teach you more than ten panicked ones.

## FAQ

**How many endings are in Dig to Escape?**
The Mineshaft and Rooftops endings are the two consistently documented routes. Community reports mention additional endings from time to time, but those two have repeatable, verifiable steps you can follow right now.

**What is the easiest ending to get first?**
Mineshaft. It requires no purchases, no keycard, and no currency grind — just a digging tool and a straight shaft down to the trigger depth.

**How much Toilet Paper do I need for the Rooftops ending?**
You need 300 Toilet Paper saved, with 200 of that spent on the Illegal Keycard. Keeping roughly 100 in reserve is a good habit in case prices shift after an update.

**Can you unlock dig to escape all endings in a single session?**
Realistically, no. The Rooftops route requires farming 300 Toilet Paper, and rushing that grind usually leads to mistakes. Plan for one ending per session — Mineshaft first, then Rooftops — and you will finish both far faster than trying to force them back to back.
