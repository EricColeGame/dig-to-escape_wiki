Every Dig to Escape run starts the same way: you spawn in a prison yard with a shovel, no money, and a guard watching your every move. The difference between a four-minute escape and a forty-minute death loop comes down to the decisions you make in your first two minutes. These dig to escape tips are built around that reality, and they're the same dig to escape tips that veteran players repeat to every new cellmate.

Roblox's prison-break loop rewards planning over raw grinding. You can spend twenty minutes digging at random and walk away with nothing, or you can follow a short checklist and slip out of the yard with time to spare. Below you'll find the class picks, the money routes, and the step-by-step escape paths that actually work — plus the habits that keep most players trapped.

## The Core Systems Behind Every Escape

Before tactics, understand what the game is really asking of you. Dig to Escape is a resource loop dressed up as a digging game. Almost everything you do feeds one of five systems.

| System | What It Does | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Digging | Uncovers dirt, tunnels, and buried loot | Your main income and the source of route progress |
| Loot & Selling | Converts found items into currency | Nothing on the escape checklist is free |
| Toilet Paper (TP) | The prison's currency | Gates every major purchase and several endings |
| Classes | Change your stats and abilities | Determines how fast you dig, move, and survive |
| Escape Routes | Multiple distinct endings | Each has different requirements and difficulty |

The key insight: digging is not the goal, it's the engine. Players who treat digging as an end in itself burn their whole shift on tunnels that lead nowhere. Players who dig with a purchase in mind finish runs. Every decision should trace back to a route and a price tag.

One more thing worth internalizing early — failure is cheap, but it isn't free. A botched escape costs you the TP you already spent, which is why the tips below always push you toward banking currency before you commit to anything.

## Picking a Class That Matches Your Route

Class choice is where most runs are won or lost. Community tier lists generally split the roster into three roles, and your intended route should decide which one you take.

| Role | What It Excels At | Best Paired With |
|---|---|---|
| Digger | Fastest dirt clearing and loot discovery | Mineshaft route, solo farming runs |
| Runner | Speed and mobility across the map | Rooftops and Kitchen routes, timed escapes |
| Utility | Opens shortcuts, supports teammates, survives guards | Janitor's route, squad play |

One of the most repeated dig to escape tips is simple: pick your class *after* you pick your route, never before. A few rules of thumb from player experience:

- Going solo and unfamiliar with the map? Take a Digger and farm first. Speed can come later.
- In a squad? Don't stack three Diggers. One digger, one runner, and one utility covers far more ground.
- Never pick a class purely for combat stats. Fighting guards is almost always slower than avoiding them.

If you want a deeper breakdown of how each ending interacts with class choice, [IGN's Dig to Escape endings guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/dig-to-escape/Endings_Guide_-_How_to_Unlock_All_Endings) is a solid companion read to the tips here.

## How to Farm Toilet Paper Fast

Toilet Paper is the bottleneck of every run. Here are the numbers that matter most, based on requirements documented across community guides.

| Purchase | Cost | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| Illegal Keycard | 200 TP | Rooftops route progress |
| Kitchen escape fund | 300 TP | Kitchen ending |
| Rooftops escape fund | 300 TP | Rooftops ending |
| General supplies | Varies | Tools, consumables, route items |

Farming principles that hold up across runs:

1. **Sell early, sell often.** Hoarding loot feels productive and isn't. Currency in your pocket buys tools that make the next dig faster.
2. **Dig in high-traffic loot zones first.** Areas near spawn and near guard patrol routes tend to have the densest item spawns — and everyone knows it, so move quickly.
3. **Chain short digs instead of one long tunnel.** Several shallow digs give you more loot rolls per minute than a single deep shaft.
4. **Bank toward a specific number.** Pick 200 or 300 TP as your target and stop farming the moment you hit it.

Here's what a disciplined farm phase looks like in practice:

| Time | Action | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–1:00 | Pick class, locate nearest loot zone | Establish income |
| 1:00–4:00 | Chain short digs, sell everything | Reach roughly 150 TP |
| 4:00–7:00 | Rotate to a second loot zone, keep selling | Reach 300 TP |
| 7:00+ | Buy your route item, move to the escape point | Escape |

That last point is the most underrated of all dig to escape tips. A run with a spending goal ends. A run without one turns into an hour of digging.

## Every Escape Route Explained

Dig to Escape ships with multiple endings, and the routes are genuinely different experiences. Before the walkthroughs, here are the dig to escape tips that apply to every route regardless of which ending you chase: fund the route before you start it, learn the final sequence, and never split your attention between two exits.

| Route | Type | Core Requirement | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mineshaft | Default | Dig to the bottom of the prison, then survive the run event | Medium |
| Janitor's | Second route | Access the janitor's room and follow its escape sequence | Medium |
| Kitchen | Currency route | Earn 300 TP, then move through the kitchen | Medium-High |
| Rooftops | Currency + item route | 300 TP saved plus the Illegal Keycard (200 TP) | High |

### Mineshaft Ending

This is the default path and the one to learn first. Dig down through the prison until you reach the bottom level, then trigger the running event and finish it. The digging isn't the hard part — the escape sequence is, because it punishes hesitation. Learn it once with a Digger class and it becomes your reliable fallback whenever a run goes sideways.

### Janitor's Ending

The janitor's route is the second escape path and it rewards players who know the map's interior. Getting into the janitor's room is the gate; everything after that is execution. Utility classes shine here because they reach areas other roles struggle with, and they're harder for guards to pin down.

### Kitchen Ending

The kitchen route is essentially a currency check. You need 300 TP banked before the kitchen becomes a viable exit. Farm with the loop above, buy what you need, and move — don't linger in the kitchen area once you're funded, because standing still is how runs die.

### Rooftops Ending

The rooftops route is the most expensive and the most demanding. You'll need 300 TP saved and 200 TP spent on the Illegal Keycard, which means a longer farming phase and more exposure to guards. Save this one until you're comfortable with the map and confident in your farm loop.

## Advanced Dig to Escape Tips for Solo and Squad Play

Once the basics are automatic, these adjustments shave real minutes off your runs.

| Situation | Adjustment | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| Solo run | Farm to target TP, then commit to one route | Splitting focus wastes both time and currency |
| Squad run | Assign one digger, one runner, one buyer | Parallel progress beats stacked roles |
| Guard-heavy map | Dig in short bursts and relocate often | Long digs are long exposure |
| Failed escape event | Reset to a known farm zone | Panic digging rarely recovers a run |

A few more habits worth building:

- **Watch the clock, not just the tunnel.** Route events get harder to complete as the round drags on.
- **Announce purchases in squads.** Two players buying the same key item is 200 TP burned for nothing.
- **Learn one route cold before learning four badly.** Mastery of a single ending beats vague knowledge of all of them.
- **Treat every guard sighting as a reroute, not a fight.** Distance is the best armor in the game.

### Mistakes That Keep Players Trapped

Most failed runs share the same handful of causes. Community reports consistently flag these:

- Digging without a destination — the single most common time sink.
- Buying tools before saving for the route item, then running out of TP.
- Ignoring class abilities and playing every role the same way.
- Panicking during the final run event instead of holding a clean line.
- Farming past the target number and losing everything to a guard.

The best dig to escape tips are usually subtractive: remove the wasted actions and the run speeds up on its own.

## FAQ

**What is the fastest route in Dig to Escape?**
The Mineshaft ending is generally the quickest to learn because it's the default path — dig to the bottom of the prison and complete the running event. Currency routes like Kitchen and Rooftops take longer because of the 300 TP requirement.

**How much Toilet Paper do I need for the Rooftops ending?**
Community guides point to 300 TP saved plus 200 TP spent on the Illegal Keycard, so budget roughly 500 TP of earning before you commit to that route.

**Which class should beginners pick?**
Start with a Digger-style class. Faster digging means faster income, and income unlocks every other route. Switch to a runner or utility class once you know the map well enough to move without thinking.

**Do these dig to escape tips work for solo players?**
Yes. Most of them are designed for solo play first — the squad advice is an add-on. Solo runs just need tighter discipline about hitting your TP target and committing to one route instead of dabbling in several.
