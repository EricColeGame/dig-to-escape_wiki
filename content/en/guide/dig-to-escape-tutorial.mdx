## Why a Good Dig to Escape Tutorial Saves You Hours

You spawn in a cell with a shovel, a rumor about a tunnel under the prison, and absolutely no instructions. That's the starting point of Dig to Escape, and it's also why so many new players spend their first hour digging in circles. A solid dig to escape tutorial matters because the game rewards efficiency far more than it rewards button-mashing — the players who understand the loop early are the ones unlocking endings while everyone else is still figuring out where to sell junk.

This guide walks through the entire progression: how digging works, how to turn loot into Toilet Paper, which class archetypes to look for, and what each ending actually demands. If you follow the dig to escape tutorial below in order, you'll skip the trial-and-error phase entirely.

## What Is Dig to Escape?

Dig to Escape is a Roblox prison simulator built around a single, ridiculous premise: you got arrested, there's supposedly a secret tunnel beneath the facility, and the only way out is straight down. Instead of picking locks or sneaking past guards, you dig. Every shovelful of dirt can surface scrap, tools, or valuables, and nearly everything you unearth has a buyer somewhere in the prison.

The currency you're chasing is Toilet Paper — yes, really. It's the game's money, and it's the gate on every escape route. Dig up items, sell them, bank the Toilet Paper, and repeat until you can afford the requirements for the ending you want.

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Platform | Roblox (PC, mobile, and console) |
| Genre | Prison escape simulator with incremental digging |
| Core currency | Toilet Paper |
| Primary activity | Dig, collect, sell, upgrade |
| Main goal | Unlock one of several endings |
| Reported costs | Community reports put a basic escape around 200 Toilet Paper and the Kitchen Ending around 300 |

The loop sounds shallow, and that's exactly the point — it's a game you can play in ten-minute bursts or grind for an entire evening. What keeps it interesting is that the endings aren't just "pay the toll." Several of them require you to complete objectives on top of the Toilet Paper cost, which is where class choice and planning start to matter.

## The Core Gameplay Loop, Step by Step

Everything in Dig to Escape funnels through four actions. Master the order and you'll never wonder what to do next.

| Stage | What You Do | Payoff | Common Pitfall |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Dig | Swing at dirt tiles to unearth items | Raw loot and materials | Digging the same exhausted spot repeatedly |
| 2. Collect | Grab everything that drops and manage your inventory | Sellable goods | Leaving loot behind because you're full |
| 3. Sell | Trade items to the vendor for Toilet Paper | Spendable currency | Hoarding items "just in case" |
| 4. Reinvest | Buy upgrades, tools, or ending requirements | Faster digging, bigger profits | Spending everything on cosmetics early |

**Stage 1 — Dig.** Start near your cell and work outward. Early digs are slow, and that's normal. Your first upgrades should always go toward dig speed or carry capacity, because both directly multiply how much Toilet Paper you earn per session.

**Stage 2 — Collect.** Inventory space is the silent bottleneck. If you're constantly running back to sell, you're losing minutes every run. Community reports suggest prioritizing capacity upgrades before cosmetic ones for exactly this reason.

**Stage 3 — Sell.** Cash out often. There's no benefit to sitting on a full inventory, and prices for common items are stable enough that timing the market isn't worth the effort.

**Stage 4 — Reinvest.** This is where most players stall. Buying a shiny cosmetic feels good for thirty seconds; buying a shovel upgrade pays for itself within a few runs.

## Toilet Paper Farming: How to Earn Faster

Toilet Paper is the only thing standing between you and an ending, so treat every session like a farming run with a target number in mind.

| Priority | Action | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| High | Empty your inventory completely each run | Converts loot to currency immediately |
| High | Upgrade dig speed before anything else | More dirt per minute equals more items |
| Medium | Move to fresh dig areas instead of re-digging | Depleted spots yield less over time |
| Medium | Play with friends and split roles | One player digs, one sells — fewer wasted trips |
| Low | Chase rare items specifically | Inconsistent returns for new players |

Set a goal before you log in. If you're aiming for the Kitchen Ending and its reported 300 Toilet Paper requirement, break it into chunks — 100 per session is a realistic target once you have a couple of upgrades. Players who grind without a number in mind tend to quit at 150 because the finish line feels invisible.

One more thing worth mentioning: sell prices and item spawns have been adjusted in past updates. If a strategy that worked last month feels sluggish now, it's probably a balance change rather than something you're doing wrong.

## Classes: Pick for Your Ending, Not the Tier List

Dig to Escape gives you a class choice, and community guides consistently note that class selection shapes which ending is easiest to reach. The catch is that class lists get rebalanced, so treat any tier list as a starting point rather than gospel.

Instead of memorizing names, compare the attributes that actually affect your run:

| Attribute | Why It Matters | Question to Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Dig speed | Determines items per minute | Can I clear a dig site quickly? |
| Carry capacity | Fewer trips to the vendor | How long before my bag fills up? |
| Sell value bonus | Multiplies every run's profit | Does this pay for itself in ten runs? |
| Mobility | Faster travel between dig sites and shop | Am I wasting time walking? |
| Survivability | Keeps you alive in contested areas | Can I dig without constant interruptions? |

If you're chasing a single ending and just want it done, pick the class that boosts digging and selling — raw throughput beats clever tricks almost every time. If you're playing with a group, let one person specialize in hauling and selling while the others dig. That division of labor is the closest thing the game has to a cheat code.

Check the in-game class menu before committing, since descriptions update alongside balance patches, and cross-reference with a recent community writeup if you're unsure.

### The Kitchen Ending, Explained Simply

The Kitchen Ending is the one most players target second, because it asks for a bit more patience than a straight cash-out. Community guides describe it as a two-part job: earn roughly 300 Toilet Paper while also working through a set of Kitchen-related objectives. The mistake people make is grinding the currency first and only then discovering the objectives, which forces a second round of work. Handle both in parallel — knock out objectives while your Toilet Paper total climbs naturally from selling loot.

### Planning Your Ending Route

Before you commit to a grind, decide whether you want to see every ending or just one. If you want them all, sequence matters: start with the cheapest ending, because the upgrades you buy along the way make every subsequent run faster. If you only care about a single ending, skip the generalist upgrades and funnel everything into the specific requirements.

## Common Mistakes That Slow New Players Down

- **Digging without a target.** No Toilet Paper goal means no way to measure progress, and progress is the entire dopamine loop here.
- **Buying cosmetics first.** They don't dig faster, carry more, or sell higher. Save them for after your first ending.
- **Ignoring the objectives.** Several endings require more than money. Read the requirements before you start saving.
- **Re-digging depleted ground.** Move on when a spot dries up instead of hoping for a lucky drop.
- **Solo grinding everything.** If you have friends who play, split digging and selling roles and your hourly rate roughly doubles.
- **Trusting an old tier list.** Balance patches change class value. Verify before you invest a whole session into a build.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I start a dig to escape tutorial run as a total beginner?**
Spawn in, dig immediately near your cell, and sell everything you find until you can afford your first dig speed or capacity upgrade. Don't wander far until your shovel is upgraded — early travel time is pure waste.

**How much Toilet Paper do you need to escape?**
Community reports put a basic escape around 200 Toilet Paper and the Kitchen Ending around 300, though other endings have their own thresholds. Treat these as targets, not guarantees, since updates can adjust them.

**What's the fastest way to farm Toilet Paper?**
Empty your inventory every run, prioritize dig speed and carry capacity upgrades, and dig in fresh areas rather than exhausted ones. Playing with a partner who handles selling cuts your downtime significantly.

**Can you unlock every ending in one playthrough?**
Most players work through endings sequentially rather than simultaneously, since each one has its own currency and objective requirements. Start with the cheapest ending, keep your upgrades, and let the momentum carry you into the next one.
