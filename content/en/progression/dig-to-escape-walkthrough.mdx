Most players lose their first ten runs in Dig to Escape to the same three mistakes: digging without a plan, selling the wrong items, and panicking the moment a guard spots them. This dig to escape walkthrough exists to fix all three at once. Below you'll find the core progression loop, a step-by-step route toward the exit, every ending route the community has documented, and the class picks that make each one dramatically easier.

Why does preparation matter so much here? Because Dig to Escape is a game of compounding advantages. Every coin spent on the right upgrade makes the next layer faster, and every trip you avoid is a trip you spend digging instead. Players who plan finish runs in a fraction of the time it takes players who dig blindly.

## How Progression Works in Dig to Escape

The game runs on a loop, not a straight line. You dig, you sell, you upgrade, then you dig deeper. Understanding that loop is what separates a twenty-minute escape from a two-hour one.

Every run starts in a cell. Your floor is your first workspace, and the layers beneath it are your income. Ore, scrap, and other diggable items fill your inventory, and the only real reason to leave your cell early is to convert that loot into coins. That single rule — leave only to sell — keeps your risk low while your bank grows steadily.

Community reports also point to free food sources, such as the kitchen area, as a way to stretch long runs without burning coins on survival items. If you can eat for free, you can spend your income on tools instead of supplies. That is a massive tempo advantage over players who treat food as a purchase.

| Stage | What you're doing | What it unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Dig | Carving out your cell floor and the layers below it | Raw ore, scrap, and sellable loot |
| 2. Sell | Leaving your cell only long enough to dump inventory | Coins for tools and gear |
| 3. Upgrade | Spending coins on pickaxe, storage, and movement | Faster digging and fewer sell trips |
| 4. Push | Extending your tunnel toward the exit route | Access to ending triggers |

The upgrade order matters more than the upgrade itself. A stronger pickaxe pays for itself within a couple of layers, while a bigger bag only pays off if you're already digging efficiently. Speed upgrades sit somewhere in between — useful, but rarely the thing holding you back early.

## Dig to Escape Walkthrough: The Main Escape Route Step by Step

This is the route most players attempt first, and it is the one that teaches you everything else the game throws at you. The short version: go down, sell often, break into the exit tunnel, then run.

According to community walkthroughs, the bottom of the dig eventually gives way to a layer of sand. Digging through that sand is what reveals the exit tunnel, which then leads into a mineshaft. From there, the run turns into a chase — you have to move through the mineshaft and climb out before a guard catches you. That final stretch is where most unprepared players lose their run.

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clear your cell floor completely before wandering | Establishes a safe, repeatable dig site |
| 2 | Follow the dirt and stone layers downward | Predictable layers mean predictable loot |
| 3 | Bank loot early and often | Coins in your pocket can't be lost to a bad encounter |
| 4 | Upgrade your pickaxe before anything else | Directly shortens every future layer |
| 5 | Keep digging until you hit the sand layer | This is the gateway to the exit tunnel |
| 6 | Break through the sand into the exit tunnel | Opens the final sequence |
| 7 | Sprint the mineshaft and climb out | The guard chase is a movement check, not a fight |

The guard chase deserves its own note. You are not meant to fight your way out. The mineshaft section rewards clean movement: know which direction the exit runs, don't stop to grab optional loot, and don't take a wrong turn because you were watching your inventory. If you die here, it's almost always a routing error rather than a skill gap.

For a second opinion on the ending triggers themselves, [IGN's Dig to Escape endings guide](https://www.ign.com/wikis/dig-to-escape/Endings_Guide_-_How_to_Unlock_All_Endings) breaks down the same exit sequence in detail.

## Every Ending Route and How to Unlock It

Dig to Escape isn't a single-finish game. Different routes lead to different endings, and a few of them require you to do something specific before the escape itself. Community guides list multiple endings, and most players find their first one by accident while chasing a tool upgrade.

The kitchen route is the clearest example of an ending that changes your routine rather than your destination. According to community coverage, you scan a door to enter the kitchen area, then keep digging for items and leaving your cell only to sell them. The free food in that area is what makes longer, riskier runs sustainable — you can stay out of your cell longer without spending coins on supplies.

| Ending route | How you reach it | Key checkpoint | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tunnel / mineshaft escape | Dig to the bottom sand layer, break into the exit tunnel, then outrun the guard | Reaching the sand layer | Medium–High |
| Kitchen route | Scan the door into the kitchen area, keep digging and selling, and use free food to sustain long runs | Scanned door into the kitchen | Medium |
| Additional endings | Community reports describe further routes tied to specific classes and items | Varies by route | Varies |

A practical tip: don't try to chain endings in one session. Pick a route, commit to it, and finish it. Learning the map for a single ending is far more efficient than half-learning three at once.

## Best Classes for Each Ending Route

Class choice is the most underrated decision in the game, and it's the one players regret most. Community guides pair specific classes with specific endings, and the logic behind those pairings is usually about what the route demands: raw digging speed, movement speed, or utility that unlocks a door you otherwise can't reach.

Rather than memorizing a tier list, learn the three jobs a class can do, then match the job to your route.

| Route demand | What it looks like in practice | Class traits to prioritize |
|---|---|---|
| Deep, long digs | Hours of tunnel work with few interruptions | High dig speed, strong tool synergy |
| Escape sequences | The mineshaft chase and similar timed runs | Movement speed, stamina, clean mobility |
| Route unlocking | Doors, areas, or triggers you can't reach otherwise | Utility abilities and access tools |
| Sustained runs | Staying out of your cell for long stretches | Survivability and resource efficiency |

If you're still learning the map, favor digging-focused classes. They smooth over the early game, which is where most runs die. Once you know the exit route by heart, switch to a movement-focused pick and shave real time off the mineshaft chase. Players who main utility classes usually do so for one specific ending — and that's a perfectly valid reason, as long as you're not running that class on a route that doesn't need it.

## Dig to Escape Walkthrough Tips That Cut Run Time

These are the habits that show up again and again in community reports from players with fast clear times. None of them require skill; they require discipline.

- **Set a sell threshold.** Decide how full your bag gets before you make a trip, and stick to it. Wandering back and forth is the single biggest time sink in the early game.
- **Never dig sideways without a reason.** Horizontal tunnels cost the same effort as vertical ones but move you further from the exit layer.
- **Learn one guard pattern before you need it.** Watch the chase route once from a safe distance so the final run isn't your first look at it.
- **Bank before you push.** Before entering a new layer or area, sell everything. Progress you can't lose is progress you keep.
- **Treat food as infrastructure.** Free food sources turn a short run into a long one, which is where the real money is.
- **Stop upgrading once the route is solved.** Extra coins don't help if the escape is already guaranteed.

| Common mistake | What it costs you | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Selling one item at a time | Dozens of wasted trips | Fill your bag, then sell in bulk |
| Ignoring the pickaxe | Every layer takes longer | Upgrade the pickaxe first, always |
| Panicking in the mineshaft | A lost run at the finish line | Walk the route once before committing |
| Hoarding coins | No tempo advantage | Spend on tools, not on nothing |
| Running the wrong class | A route you can't finish cleanly | Match class traits to route demands |

## FAQ

**Is there a single dig to escape walkthrough that covers every ending?**
No single route covers everything, but the core loop is identical across endings: dig, sell, upgrade, then push toward your chosen exit. Once you understand that loop, switching between ending routes is mostly a matter of knowing the specific trigger — like scanning the kitchen door or reaching the bottom sand layer.

**What's the fastest ending to unlock first?**
Most players find the tunnel and mineshaft escape first, since it follows naturally from digging straight down. The kitchen route is a good second target because the free food makes longer runs easier while you learn the map.

**Which class should I pick as a beginner?**
Favor a digging-focused class until you know the exit route. Dig speed smooths out the early game, and the early game is where most runs fail. Movement-focused classes become better once you're confident in the mineshaft chase.

**Do I need to fight guards to escape?**
No. Community walkthroughs treat the guard encounter as a chase, not a fight. Your job is to run the mineshaft cleanly and climb out, not to trade hits. Focus on routing and movement speed instead of combat.
